## 1. Primary Research Question & Methodological Boundaries

### Primary Research Question
> *"Does adding shooter movement information improve held-out-game prediction of basketball field-goal outcomes beyond a baseline using shot characteristics and defensive context measured at shot release?"*

### Scope and Boundaries
- **Prediction Timestamp:** Exact shot release (`startFrame`). This is strictly **release-time outcome prediction**, evaluating whether kinematic momentum at release modifies make probability under defensive contest.
- **Not a Pre-Release Decision Model:** This model does **not** evaluate whether a player should have passed, driven, or shot.
- **No Causal Claims:** All models are observational conditional probability estimators ($P(Y=1 \mid X)$). Coefficients reflect statistical association, not the causal efficacy of player movement.

## 3. Calibration Correction: Nested Cross-Fitted Platt Scaling

### Audit of Historical Vulnerability
In Phase 2, post-hoc calibration fitted a univariate logistic regression on the **same training predictions** generated by the base model. Because logistic regression already optimizes cross-entropy loss on its training data, resubstitution calibration learned near-identity parameters ($a \approx 1.0$), failing to account for generalization overconfidence and adding a $+0.000857$ log loss penalty on held-out test games.

### Implemented Correction
In Phase 3A, we established a **nested, leakage-safe cross-fitting protocol**:
1. Within each outer Leave-One-Game-Out (LOGO) fold, the outer test game is quarantined.
2. An inner 9-fold LOGO cross-validation is conducted on the 9 training games.
3. The calibrator is fitted exclusively on these cross-fitted inner validation predictions.
4. The base model is fitted on the pooled 9 outer training games.
5. The calibrator scales outer test game predictions via:
   $$\text{logit}(P_{cal}) = a \cdot \text{logit}(P_{raw}) + b$$

### Calibration Reporting Rule
**Uncalibrated outer-fold predictions remain the primary benchmark.** Nested calibrated metrics are reported as secondary diagnostics.

## 4. Controlled Velocity Experiment: Model Comparison

We evaluated four pre-specified candidate models against the M1 benchmark across identical 1,322 shot rows and 10 outer LOGO folds:
1. **M1 Benchmark:** 12 raw variables — 5 continuous (distance, shot clock, release time, dribbles before, closest defender distance) and 7 categorical (shot type, 3-point indicator, catch-and-shoot, created from paint, period, contest level, contested indicator).
2. **M1 + Speed Rel ($[t - 0.4\text{s}, t]$):** Release-lookback shooter speed over the 10 frames ($400$ ms) preceding release.
3. **M1 + Speed Early ($[t - 0.8\text{s}, t - 0.4\text{s}]$):** Earlier-window shooter speed over frames $t-20$ to $t-10$.
4. **M1 + Directional ($v_{\text{rad}} + v_{\text{tan}}$):** Decomposed velocity along the attacking hoop axis:
   - $v_{\text{rad}}$: Signed radial velocity along the vector from hoop $(-40.75, 0.0)$ to shooter (positive toward hoop).
   - $v_{\text{tan}}$: Absolute lateral speed orthogonal to the radial hoop vector.
5. **Directional Ablations:** $v_{\text{rad}}$ alone, and $v_{\text{tan}}$ alone.

## 5. Statistical Uncertainty & Cluster Bootstrap Analysis

To rigorously assess statistical significance while respecting game-level clustering, we ran a paired cluster bootstrap ($B = 2,000$ resamples) that resamples intact game clusters with replacement, preserving within-game correlations.

### Inferential Limitations of Small-Cluster Resampling
With only **10 game clusters**, cluster bootstrap confidence intervals carry inherent volatility. Shots within a game share team tactics, opponent matchups, and arena optical conditions. Treating shots as independent replicates would artificially shrink standard errors by over $3.6\times$ ($\sqrt{1322 / 10} \approx 11.5$). Resampling clusters correctly exposes this uncertainty.

### Interpretation of Statistical Results:
1. **Speed Rel Interval Spans Zero:** The 95% bootstrap interval $[-0.002177, +0.000854]$ crosses zero. While `M1 + speed_rel` improved log loss in 7 of 10 games, we cannot reject the null hypothesis of zero generalization benefit at the $\alpha = 0.05$ level.
2. **Directional Decomposition Fails Robustly:** Both directional components ($v_{\text{rad}}$ and $v_{\text{tan}}$) independently and jointly degrade held-out log loss. The bootstrap CI for `M1 + directional` is strictly positive ($[+0.000211, +0.002238]$), indicating that separating scalar speed into signed Cartesian components adds model variance that regularized logistic regression cannot support on a 10-game sample.

## 8. Final Research Verdict

### Formal Decision: **Retain M1 as the Final Validated Benchmark**

1. **Retain M1 Benchmark:** The M1 model (12 raw variables: distance, shot clock, release time, dribbles before, closest defender distance, shot type, 3-point indicator, catch-and-shoot, created from paint, period, contest level, contested indicator) remains the official, frozen CourtIQ benchmark.
2. **Retain Shooter Velocity Strictly as an Unconfirmed Exploratory Signal:** While release-lookback shooter speed demonstrates a modest empirical gain ($\Delta \text{LogLoss} = -0.000758$, 7/10 games improved), its 95% cluster bootstrap interval spans zero and fails the pre-specified $0.0020$ practical threshold.
3. **Firm Rejection of Directional Components:** Decomposing movement into radial and tangential components significantly worsens generalization error and must not be included.
4. **Recommendation for Future Data Collection:** Because statistical power is fundamentally constrained by the 10-game cluster sample ($N=10$ clusters, 1,322 shots), extending the benchmark to 30+ games is required before kinematic features can be declared validated.
